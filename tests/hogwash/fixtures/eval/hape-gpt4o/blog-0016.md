2002-11-10 - 3:02 p.m. I find myself in a strange place, caught between opposing emotions and thoughts that twist and turn like a relentless tide. On one hand, there's this undeniable draw, this inherent need, to reach out to God, to seek solace and understanding in His presence. On the other, there's an intense anger, a dissatisfaction with the way things are, the way they've been. It's like a battle within my soul, where both sides have compelling arguments, but there's no clear victor. I've been spending more time at the library lately, attempting to bury my turmoil in books and articles. I delve into stories where characters grapple with their faith, or sometimes, the lack of it. There's something comforting in realizing this struggle isn't uniquely mine. If others have traversed similar paths and found peace, perhaps I can too. Currently, I'm engrossed in "The Road Less Traveled" by M. Scott Peck. It's a challenging read, but it s insights about love, discipline, and grace resonate with me. Maybe it's what I need right now-to learn that true growth might stem from enduring what we find most difficult. Last Friday, I met with Ira for coffee. She seemed concerned, probably because my demeanor has changed so starkly these past weeks. Over our lattes, she gently probed, seeking to understand my despondency. I admitted to her my struggles with faith, my anger at God, my disdain for societal constructs that feel oppressive. She listened without judgment, offering empathy and sharing similar instances from her own life. Her patience is something I deeply admire. It struck me that this is what community is all about-supporting one another through valleys of doubt and peaks of joy. Yesterday, I attended a small group meeting at the church, something I hadn't done in quite a while. It was precisely as awkward as I expected, sitting among people who seemed so sure, so grounded in their belief, compared to my current state of disarray. Yet, there was comfort in the familiarity of their faces and voices, in the shared act of gathering to bridge our earthly struggles with divine hope. I stayed silent, absorbing the shared testimonies of deliverance, healing, and grace as they washed over me like a soothing balm. For now, it's enough to simply be present, to let the words settle and find a place within me. I feel a change stirring slowly, a gentle whisper against the hardened crust of my reservations. The desire to reconcile my path with the divine blueprint God envisions remains-a flame flickering against the darkness of my doubts. And perhaps, that's where faith thrives, in the tension between certainty and question, love and frustration. Tonight, I will try to pray, not with gritted teeth or clenched fists, but with openness, acknowledging my pain, my confusion, and my longing for clarity. Maybe that's all prayer needs to be-an honest conversation with the Creator about the messiness of being human. If I remain quiet enough, maybe I'll hear the still, small voice in the chaos, reminding me of the love that's always there, waiting to draw me back into the fold.
