# Notes on the new archive proposal

I hope this message finds you well. I wanted to share a few thoughts on the
archive proposal before the working group meets again.

The proposal sits at the forefront of a wider shift in how we keep records, and
open licensing has become a cornerstone of that shift. Volunteers now embark on
digitisation work that would once have needed a bustling department, and the
tools available to them unlock capabilities that were out of reach five years
ago. Furthermore, the reporting layer showcases insights that used to sit in a
spreadsheet nobody opened.

That reach is a double-edged sword. Wider access elevates the archive's profile,
but it also strikes a delicate balance between openness and the privacy of
living donors. The consent files we reviewed last month are only
the tip of the iceberg; several boxes have no paperwork at all.

I would greatly appreciate your guidance on the consent question, since you have
worked on these files far longer than I have. Let us work together on a short
checklist that the volunteers can follow without a lawyer in the room.

Please do not hesitate to reach out if any of this reads wrongly to you, and I
will revise it before Thursday.
